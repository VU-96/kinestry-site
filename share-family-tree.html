How to share your family tree with your family

From an Android phone, without uploading it anywhere

Most family tree apps share a tree by keeping it on their own servers and giving your relatives a login. Kinestry has no servers and no logins, so it shares the way you share a photo: you send the file yourself, to the people you choose.

The short version: open Settings, tap Share tree with family, then Copy link and share, and choose WhatsApp, email or any other app. If the download link doesn’t appear with the file, paste it into the message.

Step by step

  1. In Kinestry, open Settings and tap Share tree with family.
  2. Tap Copy link and share. Kinestry copies a link to download the app, then opens your phone’s share menu.
  3. Choose WhatsApp, email or any other app, and pick your family.
  4. If the link doesn’t appear with the file, paste it into the message. Some apps, WhatsApp among them, leave it out when you send a file. In WhatsApp, long-press the caption box and choose Paste.

What your family receives

A single file named after your tree, such as The Hartwell Family (Kinestry).json, holding the whole tree: every person, date, place and note, and the photos.

Anyone who has Kinestry taps the file and the tree opens on their phone, under the same name it has on yours. It always opens as a separate tree, so nothing they already have is overwritten or merged. If they don’t have Kinestry yet, the link tells them where to get it; it is free on Google Play.

Copies don’t stay in step

Each phone holds its own copy. If you add a cousin next month, your family’s copies won’t change; share the tree again when it has grown. It helps if one person in the family keeps the main copy and sends out the new one.

If they only want to look

Not everyone wants another app. For a relative who just wants to see the tree, export a PDF or an image from the Export button instead: it opens on any phone or computer, and prints well. How to print your family tree covers the sizes.

Who can read a shared tree

The file goes only where you send it. It never passes through a Kinestry server, because there isn’t one. But it is not encrypted or password-protected: anyone who receives it can open it, so send it only to people you trust. Once sent, a copy lives on their phone and with the app that carried it, and nobody can call it back. The privacy policy says more.

It counts as a backup too

Sharing also saves a copy on your own phone, so Settings will show Last backup: today. A backup that lives only on your phone is lost with the phone, though. Send one copy somewhere you control as well, such as your own email or drive.
